## Why People Search for the Embargo Date

A "review embargo" is the date publishers allow outlets to publish their reviews. Before it lifts, media outlets are under NDA — they can play the game but can't say anything publicly.

For players on the fence about a day-one purchase, the embargo date matters because it's the **first independent verdict** on whether the game delivers. Searching for it usually means one of two things:

- You want to read reviews before committing to a pre-order.
- You're waiting to see if launch-day bugs or performance issues are flagged early.

Control Resonant costs full retail price on PS5, Xbox, and PC, so it's a natural question. As of September 1, 2026, no official date has been announced — but the pattern is predictable.

## Remedy's Embargo History

Remedy has published three major games in the modern era. Their embargo behavior has varied, but there's a clear pattern:

| Game | Embargo → Release Gap | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| *Control* (2019, 505 Games) | ~4–5 days before release | Reviews went live the week before launch |
| *Alan Wake 2* (2023, self-published) | ~24–48 hours before release | Very tight — strong spoiler protection, reviews landed right before launch |
| *FBC: Firebreak* (2025, self-published) | Co-op title, review coverage was more staggered | Different beast — multiplayer games don't follow the single-player pattern |

The key insight: **Remedy self-published titles tend to run tighter embargoes** than the industry average. *Alan Wake 2* kept reviews close to launch, partly to protect its narrative twists. Control Resonant is a story-heavy sequel with Dylan Faden's arc at the center — don't be surprised if Remedy plays it safe again.

That said, self-published *Alan Wake 2* was a best-in-class showcase game that won multiple GOTY awards. Publishers confident in their product usually want reviews out early enough to build momentum. The September 24 launch is one of the biggest release windows of the year, and Remedy will want positive review momentum heading into it.

**Our best guess: reviews between September 17 and September 21, with the spoiler-sensitive option of September 21–22.**

## What Reviews Will Tell You (and What They Won't)

Reviews are most useful for three day-one questions:

1. **Performance**: Which platform runs best? Is the PC version optimized, or does it need patches? (This is often the deciding factor for PC buyers.)
2. **Combat depth**: Does the shape-shifting Aberrant combat live up to the *DOOM (2016)* / *Sekiro* inspirations Remedy has cited? Early previews were positive but limited to short demos.
3. **Content volume**: Main story length (~30 hours per our [playtime guide](/games/control-resonant/how-long-to-beat)), side content quality, and whether the open-world Manhattan structure holds up.

What reviews **won't** tell you: your own preference. Difficulty is [fully adjustable](/games/control-resonant/difficulty-settings), so a reviewer who dislikes the challenge might not reflect your experience — check whether the reviewer played with assists or specific settings.
