Never played *Control*? Played it once in 2019 and remember nothing? Either way, this is the starter page for Control Resonant. Remedy has confirmed the sequel is **built to stand alone** — you don't need to have finished Control, the AWE DLC, or Alan Wake 2 to follow Dylan Faden's story. But a little context makes the first few hours dramatically better.

Here are the 12 tips that matter most, from lore to combat to launch-day logistics.

## Tip 1: The Three-Second Lore Primer

Here's everything you *actually* need to know before the tutorial finishes:

- **The FBC** (Federal Bureau of Control) is a secret U.S. government agency that investigates the paranatural.
- **The Hiss** is a reality-corrupting entity that took over the FBC's building (the Oldest House) and turned people into hostile thralls.
- **Jesse Faden** is the protagonist of the first game — she became the Director of the FBC after defeating the Hiss.
- **Dylan Faden** is Jesse's brother, a former Hiss host with immense resonance powers of his own. **In Control Resonant, he's the protagonist**, and he's searching for Jesse, who has disappeared as a paranatural crisis consumes Manhattan.

That's it. The game catches you up on anything else.

## Tip 2: Read the Files — They're the Story's Skeleton

Remedy games hide their best world-building in collectible documents and audio logs. Control Resonant reportedly continues this tradition: the Federal Files scattered around warped Manhattan fill in what happened between the two games — including where Jesse went and why Dylan's resonance powers are now manifesting as a physical weapon.

You can ignore them and still understand the plot. But if you're the kind of player who likes to know *why*, grab every document you find. The [FAQ](/games/control-resonant/faq) covers the biggest questions if you want the short version.

## Tip 3: The Aberrant Is Your Whole Build — Start Form-Agnostic

Your weapon, your skill tree, and your playstyle all live in one system: the [Aberrant's five forms](/games/control-resonant/aberrant-weapon-forms) (Dual Blades, Axe, Scythe, Whip, Heavy Hammer). As a beginner, resist the urge to commit to a single form early.

For your first five hours: **default to the Scythe or Dual Blades** (forgiving speed and coverage), and swap to the **Axe or Hammer** only when an enemy is staggered. This teaches you the form-swap rhythm that the combat system is built around, and you can respec later — see Tip 7.

## Tip 4: Learn the Gap System Before It Learns You

The **Gap system** is Control Resonant's headline combat mechanic (full breakdown [here](/games/control-resonant/combat-systems-preview)). In short: enemies have a "Gap" — a stagger/break meter that fills as you land hits. When it breaks, they're open to powerful finishers.

Beginner version: **hit enemies with fast forms (Dual Blades, Whip) to build the Gap, then punish with heavy forms (Axe, Hammer).** That two-step rhythm will carry you through the first third of the game.

## Tip 5: Explore Manhattan — Vertically and Horizontally

Warped Manhattan is Remedy's biggest open area, and it's dense with rewards: ability points, weapon upgrades, lore files, and optional encounters. The early area maps aren't huge, but they hide plenty — check rooftops, basements, and anything that looks broken in an interesting way.

<InfoBox title="Side content feeds the build">
In Remedy's previous games, optional exploration was the main source of progression resources. Expect the same here: if you beeline the story, your Aberrant will lag behind the enemy scaling. Fifteen minutes of exploring between story beats keeps you on curve.
</InfoBox>

## Tip 6: Dodge Is Better Than Block — Learn the Rhythm

From what we know of the combat, Control Resonant rewards *timing* over tanking. Attacks can be dodged (or parried in some cases), and the dodge has generous iframes in Remedy's recent combat designs. Spend your first few fights practicing: attack until the enemy winds up, dodge through the swing, punish from behind.

The game's difficulty reportedly scales with how well you internalize this loop — bosses in particular are designed around it.

## Tip 7: Don't Stress About Respec — It's Cheap (or Free)

One of the most player-friendly things we know about Control Resonant: **respec is a first-class system**. Skill points invested in Aberrant forms and abilities can be redistributed, which means there are no permanently wrong choices.

Beginner takeaway: spend your points, experiment, and re-roll when you find a form combination you like. There's no reason to hoard points out of fear — the [combat systems preview](/games/control-resonant/combat-systems-preview) has the details.

## Tip 8: Upgrade One Form Line, Then Diversify

Even with respec available, focus is more efficient early. Pick **one primary form** (Scythe is our beginner recommendation) and pour the first chunk of your upgrades into its tree. This keeps your damage curve healthy while you learn the game. Branch out only after you've cleared the early-mid game hump.

## Tip 9: Use the Whip Against Things You Don't Want to Touch

Flying enemies, shielded enemies, and anything with a dangerous melee attack: the **Whip form** is your answer. It's the Aberrant's ranged option, and it lets you learn enemy patterns from a safe distance. Beginners who ignore the Whip end up eating attacks they could have outsourced to a lash from ten meters away.

## Tip 11: Difficulty Is a Setting, Not a Trophy

Remedy's games ship with multiple difficulty options, and Control Resonant reportedly follows suit — including options to reduce combat challenge for players who want the story. There is no honor in suffering through a game you could be enjoying. Start on Normal; adjust when it stops being fun.

## Tip 12: Play the First Two Hours With Sound On

This sounds like a joke; it isn't. Remedy's audio design — and Control Resonant's resonance-based identity — means half the atmosphere is auditory. The warped Manhattan's audio cues also carry gameplay information (enemy types, attack windups, resonance surges). Headphones on, first session, no distractions.

## Your First Session, Summarized

Here's the shape of a good first two hours:

1. Play the opening sequence with sound on (Tip 12).
2. Don't skip the tutorial's form-swap lesson (Tips 3–4).
3. Follow the story until you reach the first open area (Tip 5).
4. Spend 20 minutes exploring, grab every file you find (Tips 2, 5).
5. Fight with the Scythe, punish with the Axe (Tips 3–4).
6. End the session with a plan for which form to main (Tip 8).

## FAQ

### Do I need to play Control before Control Resonant?
No. Remedy has confirmed Control Resonant is built to stand alone. The game introduces Dylan's story and the setting on its own, though playing Control enriches the experience.

### Who is the protagonist of Control Resonant?
Dylan Faden, Jesse Faden's brother and a former Hiss host with immense resonance powers. He wields the Aberrant weapon.

### Is Control Resonant good for newcomers to action RPGs?
Yes — it reportedly includes multiple difficulty options, a forgiving dodge, cheap respec, and a combat system that teaches you one rhythm at a time.

### What's the best starting weapon form?
The Scythe is the most forgiving generalist for new players. Pair it with the Axe for staggered-enemy punishes and the Whip for ranged problems.

### How long is Control Resonant?
Around 30 hours for the main story, and about 50 hours to see everything, according to lead designer Sergey Mokhov.

## The Bottom Line

Control Resonant welcomes newcomers with open arms and a shapeshifting weapon. Learn the two-step rhythm (fast forms build the Gap, heavy forms punish it), explore between story beats, and don't fear respec. Six weeks out from launch, that's the whole starter kit — the rest is practice.
