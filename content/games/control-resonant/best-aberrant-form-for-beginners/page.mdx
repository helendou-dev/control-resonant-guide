**Short answer: Cleave is the best Aberrant primary form for beginners in Control Resonant, paired with Extend as your secondary.** Cleave's attack strings are the most forgiving in the game, and Extend's whip reach lets you learn enemy patterns without standing inside the danger zone. It's the pairing that covers your mistakes while the combat system clicks.

This page explains how the real primary + secondary loadout system works, ranks all six forms by beginner-friendliness, and gives you a simple unlock path so you're not wasting early Living Shards.

> **Correction (Oct 4):** An earlier version of this page recommended "Dual Blades" from a pre-launch "5 forms" guess. That form system doesn't exist in the shipped game. The real weapon has **six forms in primary/secondary slots** — this page now reflects the actual game.

## How the Weapon System Actually Works

Before picking forms, you need the shape of the system:

- You equip **one primary form + one secondary form** at a time, in your Loadout
- **Primaries** (Strike / Glaive / Cleave) are your main attack moveset
- **Secondaries** (Crush / Extend / Drill) are a distinct offhand attack you weave in
- Each pairing grants one of six **Combo Finishers** — your biggest damage windows
- New forms are bought with **Living Shards** (three for a primary, two for a secondary) and upgraded in **The Gap**

So "best form for beginners" is really "best *pairing* for beginners" — you're choosing two forms, not one. Full details in our [Aberrant forms tier list](/games/control-resonant/aberrant-weapon-forms).

## What Makes a Form "Beginner-Friendly"?

Three factors matter more than raw damage when you're learning the game:

1. **Forgiveness** — how punished you are for a missed attack or a mistimed dodge. Remember, Resonant has **no block and no healing items**: you dodge or you get hit, and only kills heal you. Recovery speed is everything.
2. **Coverage** — how many situations the pairing handles without demanding execution skills. You shouldn't need frame-perfect timing on day one.
3. **Learning value** — how fast the form teaches you the core loop: build Falter, break the enemy, land the finisher.

## Why Cleave Is the #1 Pick

**1. It solves the "no block" problem.** Resonant's combat has no block — a mistimed exchange means damage, and you don't heal without kills. Cleave's short recovery on every attack means a whiff leaves you able to dodge immediately. It's the primary that charges you the least for mistakes.

**2. It works everywhere.** Bosses, swarms, elites — Cleave never feels outclassed, even if it never tops a damage chart. New players change encounters constantly; a form with no bad matchup removes one variable from a game that already has many.

**3. It builds good habits.** Cleave's backstab bonus directly rewards dodge-*through* repositioning — the exact skill the endgame demands. You'll graduate to Strike's crit strings or Crush's Falter loops already knowing how to move.

<ProTip title="The beginner loop">
Run **Cleave + Extend** and use this pattern: poke from range with Extend to learn the tells, engage with Cleave's forgiving strings, dodge through to the enemy's back for the bonus, and cash out Falter windows with your Combo Finisher. Every piece of that loop transfers to the harder pairings later.
</ProTip>

## The Easiest Alternative: Glaive + Extend

If even Cleave feels too busy, **Glaive + Extend** is the maximum-safety pairing. Glaive's wide sweeping arcs hit everything in front of you — whiffing is nearly impossible — while Extend keeps pulling threats out of formation. It's slower, it won't win boss DPS races, but it will carry you through the campaign's mob-heavy zones with minimal stress. Trade up to Cleave once enemy attack patterns feel readable.

## When to Graduate to the Hard Forms

Strike and Crush are the *real* damage dealers — Strike's crit strings melt bosses, and Crush has the highest Falter application in the game. But they're punish tools: they reward knowing exactly when an enemy's attack whiffs.

**Switch to them when you can answer "yes" to both:**
- Can you reliably identify each enemy's big telegraphed attack and dodge it?
- Do you know when a Falter window is coming before it happens?

If not, stay on Cleave/Extend — and remember the game's [free respec](/games/control-resonant/combat-systems-preview) means experimenting with harder forms later costs nothing.

## A Simple Beginner Unlock Path

1. **Start:** You begin with a starter primary. Main-line it — Cleave is your first Living Shard target (three shards) if it's not your starter.
2. **First secondary (two shards):** Buy **Extend**. The ranged control covers every primary's weakness and teaches enemy patterns safely.
3. **Mid-game:** Pick your direction — **Strike** (three shards) if boss damage is the goal, or **Glaive** if you're still drowning in crowds.
4. **Endgame:** Add **Crush** or **Drill** for the Falter/debuff loops, and pour The Gap currency into the pairing you actually run. The [difficulty options](/games/control-resonant/difficulty-settings) are there if you want a gentler or spicier ride.

## FAQ

### What is the best Aberrant form for beginners?
**Cleave**, paired with **Extend** — the most forgiving primary and the safest secondary. Glaive + Extend is the maximum-safety alternative if crowd control is your comfort zone.

### Is Strike good for beginners?
Not as a first form. Strike has the highest damage ceiling of the primaries, but its melee-range crit strings demand tight dodge timing in a game with no block and no healing items. Pick it up once enemy tells are readable.

### How many forms can I equip at once?
Two: **one primary + one secondary** in your Loadout. New forms cost Living Shards (three for a primary, two for a secondary) and are upgraded in The Gap.

### Can I change my Aberrant weapon build later?
Yes — the [free respec](/games/control-resonant/combat-systems-preview) lets you redistribute skill points at safe zones, and form loadouts can be swapped freely, so experimenting costs nothing.

### Does the best beginner form change at higher difficulty?
At [higher difficulty tiers](/games/control-resonant/difficulty-settings), enemies punish harder and healing-on-kill gets riskier, which makes Extend's safe range *relatively* stronger and pushes Cleave further ahead of Strike as the beginner main.

## The Bottom Line

Pick **Cleave**, keep **Extend** at your side, and let the forgiving strings teach you the game — the pairing's job is to make you *learn* combat without getting wrecked while doing it. Once Falter windows are second nature, Strike's crit strings and Crush's knockdowns will be waiting, and the [free respec](/games/control-resonant/combat-systems-preview) means you never pay for a wrong guess.
