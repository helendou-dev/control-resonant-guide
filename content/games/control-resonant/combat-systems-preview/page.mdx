The combat in Control Resonant is the most talked-about part of the game, and for good reason: it's a real departure from the first *Control*. The Service Weapon's gunplay has been replaced by the **Aberrant's five melee forms**, and the new **Gap system** restructures how fights flow from open to finish. Add in a build system with free respec and a proper New Game+, and you've got the most RPG-forward combat Remedy has ever shipped.

This preview collects everything confirmed or strongly indicated about the systems — we'll update it as official breakdowns land before September 24.

## The Core Loop: The Gap System

The Gap system is the skeleton of Control Resonant's combat. Here's how it works in plain terms:

1. **Every enemy has a Gap meter** — a stagger/break bar that depletes as you land hits.
2. **Fast, frequent hits** (Dual Blades, Whip) drain the Gap quickly but do modest damage.
3. **When the Gap breaks**, the enemy is staggered and vulnerable — this is your window.
4. **Heavy forms** (Axe, Heavy Hammer) deal massive bonus damage to staggered enemies, and the game's **finishers** trigger off broken-Gap enemies.

The rhythm is deliberate: *chip with fast forms, punish with heavy forms, execute with finishers*. Remedy has leaned hard into this loop in the gameplay shown so far, and it's clearly the thing the combat system is built to teach you.

<ProTip title="The two-step rule">
As a rule of thumb: you should never be dealing damage with the wrong form. If the Gap is full, you're in the chip phase (fast forms). If the Gap is broken, you're in the punish phase (heavy forms). The skill ceiling is entirely about how quickly you swap — and form-swapping is instant and free.
</ProTip>

### Gap vs. Armor

Some enemies — mostly elites and mini-bosses — have an **armor layer** on top of the Gap meter. Armor behaves differently:

- Armor **reduces damage** from all attacks until it's broken.
- The **Heavy Hammer** is the dedicated armor-break form; its charged attacks shatter armor segments.
- The **Axe** staggers through armor, buying you time even when you can't break it.

Plan your loadout around the enemy roster: armored packs demand Hammer, unarmored crowds demand Dual Blades, and dangerous singles demand Axe stagger windows.

## No Healing Items: You Heal by Killing

The single most aggressive design decision confirmed so far: **there are no healing items in combat.** Speaking during a gamescom hands-off presentation (as reported by GamesRadar), Remedy's senior communications manager Miika Huttunen confirmed that Dylan is unable to use healing items mid-fight — instead, **you recover lost health by eliminating enemies**.

What this means in practice:

- **Retreating to heal is not an option.** If your health drops, the only way back up is through the enemy in front of you.
- **Aggression is the survival mechanic.** The kill-to-heal loop stacks directly on top of the execution system (executions temporarily boost melee damage), rewarding players who chain kills instead of playing safe.
- **Glass-cannon pressure is intentional.** Previews describe Dylan as constantly dodging incoming fire — the no-healing rule is what makes that dodge-dance matter on every engagement.

<InfoBox title="First boss confirmed: The Collective">
The same gamescom presentation revealed the game's first shown boss — **The Collective**, a multi-phase fight against a mountain of Mold-formed faces that summons waves of smaller enemies while you break specific points on the entity. Phased weak-point design plus constant add pressure suggests bosses will test the kill-to-heal loop harder than any regular encounter.
</InfoBox>

## Mold Powers: A One-Choice Commitment

Dylan's ability progression includes at least one **permanent choice**: after defeating The Collective, he's offered new powers based on the Mold — **channeling the substance through his arm to attack**, or **a Mold tentacle that erupts from the ground and attacks enemies independently**.

The catch, confirmed in the same presentation: **you can only choose one of the two for the entire playthrough.** The other option only becomes available via New Game+, where existing powers carry over — letting you combine both on a second cycle.

This is the one known place where Control Resonant asks you to commit without a respec escape hatch, and it's the strongest argument yet for planning a NG+ run.

## The Aberrant & Form-Based Builds

Your build in Control Resonant is expressed through the Aberrant. The system works in two layers:

**1. Form mastery** — Each of the five forms (Dual Blades, Axe, Scythe, Whip, Heavy Hammer) has its own upgrade tree. Investing in a form improves its damage, unlocks new moves, and extends combo potential. See the full form breakdown in the [Aberrant weapon guide](/games/control-resonant/aberrant-weapon-forms).

**2. Resonance abilities** — Dylan's paranatural powers sit alongside the weapon: defensive abilities, area effects, and utility tools that complement your form choice. The interaction between a form tree and your resonance loadout is where "builds" actually get built.

### Three Build Archetypes (Early Theory)

Based on the system profiles, here are the build shapes players are planning:

| Archetype | Primary Form | Resonance Focus | Playstyle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Resonator** | Scythe | Mobility + AoE | Constant pressure, long combos, never stop moving |
| **The Breaker** | Heavy Hammer | Armor shred + defense | Tanky, deliberate, shatter everything |
| **The Staggerer** | Dual Blades / Whip | Status + gap drain | Fast chip, break Gaps constantly, finisher farming |

These are theory builds — expect concrete meta picks within the first two weeks after launch. Respec makes experimenting free, so there's no penalty for trying all three.

### Combat Inspirations (Confirmed by Remedy)

Senior level designer **Arhi Makkonen** told Wccftech during gamescom that the melee system took cues from a deliberate mix of sources:

- **FromSoftware** — specifically *Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice* and *Elden Ring* (defensive reads, posture-style mechanics)
- **Devil May Cry 2 & 3** — the aerial combo system ("you can stay in the air a lot")
- **PlatinumGames** titles broadly — for action-game readability and enemy tells
- The team's own iteration on top, which Makkonen described as "quite unique" and "not similar to anything I've seen before"

Dylan's aerial combos and the dodge-as-only-defense rule both read as direct outputs of this inspiration mix.

### A "Loop, Not a Spiral" — Resonant's Combat Design Philosophy

In a separate gamescom interview, GamesRadar asked Makkonen how the combat evolution relates to Remedy's recent storytelling experiments — particularly *Alan Wake 2*'s famous "spiral" narrative (a story you revisit in loops, but each pass deepens the lore rather than resetting it). Makkonen pushed back on the analogy directly:

> "It's a loop, not a spiral. The combat loop resets every fight — you don't carry the Gap state between encounters, you don't carry combo timers, you don't carry form choice. What carries over is *you*: better execution, better form swapping, better reading of tells. The spiral is in the world — every Resonant Cycle resets the *map*, not the *fight*."

The quote is worth pausing on because it cleanly explains two design decisions that confuse early previews:

- **Per-fight combat is a clean reset.** You can't "save up" Gap-staggered enemies between encounters, can't carry a finisher buff into the next room, can't pre-buff an enemy before the fight starts. Each engagement is a pure expression of *that moment's* execution. This is what makes the kill-to-heal loop feel honest — every drop of health you recover is earned in *that* fight, not banked from *the last* one.
- **The long arc is the spiral.** Resonant Cycle resets the *world* (more content, harder threats, changed vendors) after every Resonant boss — but the *moment-to-moment combat* doesn't have a long arc. The deep progression is in how you read tells and swap forms, not in stacking buffs across the whole game.

It's a subtle distinction that has big gameplay implications: don't expect a "build up for the big fight" structure. The boss right in front of you is the only boss that matters right now.

## Respec: Confirmed Player-Friendly

One of the most welcome confirmations: **respec is a first-class system in Control Resonant.** Skill points invested in form trees and resonance abilities can be redistributed at safe points, and the system is designed to encourage experimentation.

What we know:
- Respec cost appears to be **minimal to free** — Remedy has emphasized it as a "try everything" tool.
- It's tied to safe zones (the sequel's equivalent of Control's safe rooms) rather than consumable items.
- **Form mastery resets, but nothing else is lost** — explored areas, collected files, and cosmetic unlocks are permanent.

<InfoBox title="Why this matters for launch">
Free respec means the day-one "meta" doesn't punish early adopters. If the community discovers two weeks in that the Axe is secretly the best form, you can pivot instantly. Pre-launch, the only real decision is *which form to learn first* — and you can always change course.
</InfoBox>

## New Game+: What Carries Over

Control Resonant launches with **New Game+**, and Remedy has confirmed the loop is built around replayability. Here's what's reported to carry over between cycles:

- **Aberrant form mastery** — your weapon skill carries over, letting you start NG+ with your full form kit.
- **Resonance abilities** — all unlocked powers persist.
- **Explored map & collectibles** — already-found files and upgrades stay discovered.
- **New rewards** — NG+ reportedly adds exclusive form skins and a higher difficulty curve tuned for fully-leveled builds.

What does **not** carry over: story progression and any quest-gated unlocks. NG+ is a power-fantasy loop, not a "continue your save" mode.

### Is NG+ Worth It?

If the length profile holds (~30h main story, ~50h completionist), a full NG+ cycle puts you at 60–100 hours total. For players who enjoyed the first *Control*'s post-game content, the NG+ loop with a complete Aberrant is the designed endgame — and the exclusive rewards give completionists a concrete goal.

## Extraction Zones: The Open World, Properly Named

The "open area" in Control Resonant has a proper name in the build previews have seen: **Extraction Zones** (per a 3-hour hands-on at PlayStation London's London HQ, reported by In Game News). These are the warped Manhattan districts Dylan moves between — not a seamless open world, but a series of large, open-ended districts that host side quests, NPC interactions, and side activities (escort soldiers, clear Hiss-infested blocks, etc.).

Each Extraction Zone runs on a **Zone Level** system that climbs as you complete activities inside it:

- **Higher Zone Level → better vendors** in that district's safe zones
- **More bounties** posted as the zone levels up
- **Increased currency rewards** for activities within a higher-level zone

The practical implication: **spending time in a single Extraction Zone pays compounding dividends.** If you skip straight to the next story beat, your vendors and rewards lag behind. This is the post-launch activity loop question partly answered — and the design pushes the same "clear the map as you go" approach Remedy used in *Control (2019)* and *Alan Wake 2*.

### The World Opens Up Fast

Senior level designer Arhi Makkonen confirmed in the same Wccftech gamescom interview that **within the first 1–2 hours, the game unlocks multiple Extraction Zones at once** and players are free to pick which to explore first. Side quests are "narrative-heavy and surprising," with roughly equal side and main content density across every zone.

### No Day/Night Cycle — By Lore Design

There is no day/night simulation in Resonant's Manhattan. Each Extraction Zone has its own fixed weather and time of day that "always stay the same" (Makkonen, Wccftech). This is canon, not a technical limitation: the zones are paranaturally frozen in their own moment, part of the same reality-warping that turned Manhattan inside out.

## Resonant Cycle: The Boss-Driven World Reset

The most distinctive piece of **world-state progression** in Control Resonant, confirmed by Makkonen: the **Resonant Cycle**. After you defeat a Resonant boss, the game **resets or upgrades the world** — more things appear in the affected zone, the threats get harder, and the world is genuinely different on a second pass.

This is a separate progression axis from your character build:

- **Your Aberrant form** is upgraded through skill points and mastery.
- **Your Resonance abilities** are unlocked by defeating Resonant bosses (one ability per boss, per Makkonen).
- **The world itself** is reshaped by the Resonant Cycle after each boss kill.

The practical implication: **bosses aren't just boss fights — they're world-mutation events.** The map you see after your third Resonant Cycle is meaningfully different from the map you started with, and zone content / vendor offerings shift in response.

## Combo Enders: The Aberrant's Hidden Sixth Form

Hands-on previews have surfaced a combat layer the official materials don't talk about yet: **Combo Enders** (reported in a 3-hour hands-on published by Alpha Squad). During the final sequence of a melee combo, the Aberrant can morph into a one-off special weapon for a single hit. Two enders seen in previews:

- **Gauntlets** — rapid-fire punches in a short burst, ideal for chasing a broken Gap into a finisher.
- **Glaive-like projectile** — a spinning blade that orbits Dylan, hitting multiple targets in a crowd.

Combo Enders layer on top of the existing form-swap loop, giving players another knob to express skill — and they reinforce the same "instant weapon morphing on demand" design the Aberrant is built around. Expect more enders to be revealed before launch, since the official materials haven't enumerated them yet.

<ProTip title="The full combat loop, end to end">
Here's the confirmed cycle, top to bottom: **land melee hits → build the Gap → break the Gap → trigger finisher / execution → boost melee damage briefly → land more hits → repeat.** Every successful hit also drops blue crystals that restore Dylan's combat ability meter (for Resonance abilities), so aggression and ability use are on the same feedback loop — not separate systems.
</ProTip>

## Enemy Roster: The Hiss, The Mold, and The Patterning

Three paranatural threats operate in Resonant's Manhattan:

- **The Hiss** (returning) — completely redesigned for the sequel: more aggressive, more melee-focused. The original Control leaned on ranged Hiss units; here they're up close and personal, with new larger variants.
- **The Mold** (returning) — also redesigned; the visual identity is more organic and bubbly, and it spreads across environments in ways the first game only hinted at.
- **The Patterning** (new) — a third paranatural threat introduced in Resonant, distinct from both the Hiss and the Mold. The Patterning is the lore driver behind why Manhattan's zones have their own frozen weather and time of day — and probably the engine behind the Resonant Cycle boss events. No deep gameplay specifics yet, but Makkonen confirmed it's "a big part" of what's happening to the city.

Worth noting: every Resonant boss you defeat in the Resonant Cycle "unlocks new things in the world" (per Makkonen) — so The Patterning, the bosses, and the world-mutation events are all part of the same progression system.

## FAQ

### What is the Gap system in Control Resonant?
The Gap system is the combat's stagger mechanic. Enemies have a Gap meter that depletes as you land hits; when it breaks, they're staggered and vulnerable to finishers and heavy-form punishes. Fast forms drain the Gap, heavy forms exploit the break.

### Can I respec my build in Control Resonant?
Yes — respec is a confirmed first-class system, reportedly free or near-free, tied to safe zones. You can redistribute form mastery and resonance ability points freely.

### Is there New Game+ in Control Resonant?
Yes. New Game+ carries over Aberrant form mastery, resonance abilities, and map/collectible progress, and adds exclusive rewards on repeat cycles.

### Can I use healing items during combat in Control Resonant?
No — there are no healing items in combat. You recover lost health by eliminating enemies, as confirmed during Remedy's gamescom presentation. Aggression is the survival mechanic, and Assist Mode's survival toggles are the safety net if the loop proves too punishing.

### What is the Mold power choice in Control Resonant?
After the first boss, Dylan chooses between channeling Mold through his arm or summoning an autonomous Mold tentacle — and the choice is permanent for the whole playthrough. Only New Game+ lets you carry powers over and combine both.

### Which form is best for building the Gap meter?
Dual Blades and Whip are the fastest Gap-draining forms. Use them in the chip phase, then swap to Axe or Hammer for the punish phase.

### How do the Aberrant forms and builds interact?
Each of the five forms has its own upgrade tree, and resonance abilities sit alongside the weapon. Your "build" is the combination of a form mastery focus plus a resonance ability loadout — and respec makes experimenting free.

### What are Extraction Zones in Control Resonant?
Extraction Zones are the open-ended Manhattan districts that host side activities between story beats. Each one runs on a Zone Level that climbs as you clear tasks, unlocking better vendors, more bounties, and higher currency rewards. Spending time in a zone is the main way to stay ahead of the difficulty curve. The world opens up to multiple zones within 1–2 hours of starting the game.

### What are Combo Enders in Control Resonant?
Combo Enders are one-off special weapons the Aberrant morphs into during the final sequence of a melee combo — gauntlets for rapid punches, a spinning glaive for crowd damage, and likely more to be revealed. They layer on top of form-swap and finishers as another skill knob.

### What is the Resonant Cycle in Control Resonant?
The Resonant Cycle is the boss-driven world-reset system: each Resonant boss you defeat "resets or upgrades" the affected zone, with more content appearing, harder threats, and changed vendor offerings. It's a separate progression axis from your character build, and it makes bosses feel like world-mutation events, not just combat encounters.

### What is The Patterning in Control Resonant?
The Patterning is the third paranatural threat in Resonant, alongside the Hiss and the Mold. It's the lore driver behind Manhattan's frozen weather and time-of-day in each zone, and it's closely tied to the Resonant Cycle. No deep gameplay specifics yet — Remedy has only confirmed it as "a big part" of the world.

### What does "loop, not a spiral" mean for combat?
Senior level designer Arhi Makkonen told GamesRadar (gamescom interview) that Resonant's combat follows a "loop, not a spiral" philosophy — meaning every individual fight is a clean reset (no carry-over of Gap state, combo timers, or form buffs between encounters), while the *long arc* (Resonant Cycle, world mutation, boss ability unlocks) is what spirals upward. The deep progression is in your execution within a fight, not in stacking buffs across the whole game.

## The Bottom Line

Control Resonant's combat is a stagger-and-punish loop built around the Aberrant's instant form-swapping, wrapped in a flexible build system with free respec, meaningful New Game+, and now a proper named open-world structure (Extraction Zones) with its own progression layer. The Resonant Cycle ties boss kills to world-state mutation, and The Patterning is the third paranatural force driving both the story and the city's frozen time. Combo Enders and the hit-to-ability-resource loop give skilled players extra knobs without breaking the "form swap + break Gap + execute" foundation.
