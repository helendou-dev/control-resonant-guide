## What Reviewers Agree On (The Consensus)

Across the 84 average, **four pillars are nearly universal**:

1. **Art direction and world design is generation-defining.** VGC, GameSpot, IGN, Push Square, Eurogamer, and Polygon all lead with it — even the harshest reviews concede this is one of the best-looking games of the year. "Trailers have hinted at some of the arresting art design Resonant features, but it's truly something special to behold" (GameSpot).
2. **Boss encounters are standout.** The Resonant cycle bosses — multi-phase set pieces built around environmental shifts — are the single highest-praised combat element. "Both the world and the enemies, especially the bosses, are nothing short of jaw-dropping" (GameSpot).
3. **Accessibility options earn across-the-board praise.** The one-hit kills toggle, difficulty sliders, and assist modes drew compliments even from critical reviews — a rarity for hardcore-leaning outlets.
4. **Zoe de Vera and Dylan/Zoe chemistry.** GameSpot named Remy's *Control* newcomer as a personal favorite, calling her the relationship that "actually drives the story's appeal" rather than the paranatural spectacle.

Two secondary consensus points: **storytelling is more conventional than usual for Remedy** (even positive reviewers note this), and **length lands around 25–30 hours for the main story** — Insider Gaming and GamerHub confirm credits roll around 25 hours, with completionist runs running significantly longer.

## What Divides Critics (The Real Story)

The 92% positive average hides a **sharply polarized reception** under the hood. Three fault lines split reviewers:

### 1. The Melee Combat Question

The single biggest divide. Positive outlets call it "responsive stylish-action foundationally sound" (IGN) with "fluid weapon-form swaps" (VGC). Negative outlets call it "bafflingly dull," "no parry," "no real combo system," and "just mashing, mashing, mashing" (ComicBook). The DMC / Bayonetta / God of War comparison is where this lands hardest — for action-game traditionalists, Resonant **doesn't give you enough tools to express yourself**.

### 2. The Open-Zone Pivot

Original *Control* was a Metroidvania-style single building (the Oldest House). Resonant moves to **open zones** across Manhattan. Eurogamer is the sharpest critic here: "Remedy rebukes the idea of Control Resonant being an open-world game, and it's not quite as freewheeling… but Resonant feels like it's chasing the hallmarks of a live-service game." Polygon agrees on the "padding out its open world with rote activities" critique. Positive outlets (VGC, GameSpot) read the same zones as "unlike anything I've ever seen" — same game, same zones, radically different interpretation.

### 3. Dylan vs. Jesse as Protagonist

Reviewers who clicked with Dylan call him "a real sad sack who feels sorry for himself — tragic and easy to root for" (IGN). Reviewers who didn't call him "a black hole of charisma" and "not done justice replacing Jesse as the lead" (ComicBook, Polygon). The split isn't about Dylan's writing quality — it's about whether **the player wanted to follow a new protagonist at all** after seven years of attachment to Jesse Faden.

**What this means for buyers:** the consensus is "great world, great bosses, divisive combat." If you loved *Control*'s tight single-building design and Jesse's protagonist voice, you're in the "wait and see" zone. If you wanted *Control* to expand into something more ambitious and traversal-focused, you're in the "pre-order now" zone.
