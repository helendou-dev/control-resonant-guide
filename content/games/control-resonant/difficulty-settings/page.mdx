**Short answer: Control Resonant uses a granular "Adjustable Difficulty" system rather than a fixed Easy / Normal / Hard ladder.** According to the official PlayStation Store listing, players can "customise the level of challenge or activate a range of assists individually to help you play the game" — meaning you tune the experience to your taste rather than picking a single preset.

That's a meaningful choice. Remedy's [lead designer Sergey Mokhov has confirmed New Game+](/games/control-resonant/how-long-to-beat) carries over Aberrant progress and bumps difficulty, and creative director Mikael Kasurinen has named *DOOM (2016)* and *Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice* as combat inspirations — so the game can absolutely hit hard when you want it to. But it also won't lock you out of the story.

This page breaks down what's officially confirmed, how it likely builds on *Control (2019)*'s Assist Mode, and which settings to pick based on how you want to play.

## What's Officially Confirmed

The PlayStation Store page for Control Resonant lists two gameplay accessibility features that directly affect difficulty:

| Feature | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Adjustable Difficulty (Advanced)** | "You can customise the level of challenge or activate a range of assists individually." |
| **Game Pausing** | "You can pause the game at any time during gameplay or cinematics (offline play only)." |

The "Advanced" tag is Sony's classification for the most flexible tier of accessibility — it means the system goes well beyond a simple Easy / Normal / Hard toggle. Combined with Remedy's track record (more on that below), this strongly suggests a **slider-and-toggle system** where you can dial in enemy health, damage output, resource drops, and similar parameters independently.

That's now confirmed. At the gamescom media presentation (August 26, reported by 4Gamer), Remedy clarified the design: Control Resonant has **no traditional difficulty presets at all** — there's a **single default difficulty**, and all challenge tuning flows through the **Assist Mode**, which ships with playstyle presets and individually adjustable parameters (power recovery speed, damage taken, and whether enemy attacks can kill you — each tunable up *or* down). If you want the game harder, you use the same menu.

One combat-design note from the same presentation that shapes how difficulty feels: **there is no guard and no parry**. Dodging is the core defensive move — and a correctly timed dodge (unlocked as a talent) slows time and boosts your damage. Difficulty in Resonant is about reading attacks and reacting, not blocking them.

<InfoBox title="Also worth knowing">
Kasurinen has confirmed that **many bosses are optional** — players who struggle with a particular encounter won't be gated from continuing the main story. Optional encounters can also be tackled out of order, so you can always come back later with a stronger build.
</InfoBox>

## How It Likely Builds on Control (2019)

*Control* shipped with an **Assist Mode** accessible from the settings menu. It let players tweak:

- **Damage dealt** multiplier (1x → 2x → higher)
- **Damage taken** multiplier (1x → 0.5x → lower)
- **Energy recovery** speed (for Launch and other powers)
- **Ammo replenishment** rate
- **Immortality** toggle (no death from combat)

Each could be adjusted independently, and the game explicitly told players there was **no penalty, no achievement lock, no "cheater" flag** for using them. Remedy's design philosophy was clear: the story is for everyone, the challenge is for those who want it.

Given that the PS5 Store explicitly calls Control Resonant's difficulty "Advanced" and mentions "a range of assists individually," it's a safe bet that this same philosophy carries forward — expanded and refined for a bigger, more RPG-leaning game.

<ProTip title="Don't be proud — use the assists">
If you're here because combat feels too punishing, turn on the assists. Remedy has explicitly designed the game so that **using accessibility options is not "cheating."** The story, lore, and world are the point for many players, and the devs want you to see them.
</ProTip>

## Recommended Settings by Playstyle

Since the system is granular, think of these as starting points to dial in:

### 📖 Story-First Players

**Goal:** Experience the narrative, lore, and spectacle without friction.

- Set difficulty to its lowest tier or activate the "Story" assist preset if available
- Enable **damage taken reduction** (0.5x or lower)
- Consider **immortality toggle** for boss encounters
- Keep **damage dealt** at default so combat still feels weighty

This is also the right call if you're [new to Control](/games/control-resonant/story-recap) and want to learn the world before committing to a harder run.

### ⚖️ Balanced Players (Recommended for First Run)

**Goal:** Challenging enough to stay engaged, forgiving enough to enjoy exploration.

- Default difficulty (whatever the middle tier is)
- Leave assists off unless you hit a wall
- Swap Aberrant weapon forms freely — the [five forms](/games/control-resonant/aberrant-weapon-forms) are designed to cover different combat scenarios

Most players will land here. If you're spending more than ~3 attempts on a standard encounter, drop the difficulty one notch — the game is 30 hours long and your time is worth more than stubbornness.

### 🔥 Challenge Seekers

**Goal:** Sweat every fight, earn every win.

- Highest available difficulty from the start
- No assists
- Min-max your build: invest in the skill tree, assign trinkets with trade-offs (more damage taken in exchange for more damage dealt)
- Save NG+ for after credits roll — it carries over your build and raises the ceiling further

Kasurinen's *Sekiro* reference means parry timing, dodge windows, and enemy aggression will likely punish hesitation. If you love that, crank it up.

## New Game+ Difficulty

Mokhov has confirmed that **New Game+ is in Control Resonant**, and it comes with a higher difficulty baseline. Here's what carries over:

- ✅ Aberrant weapon upgrades and mastery progress
- ✅ Paranatural abilities and skill-tree investments
- ✅ Artifacts and collected trinkets
- ❌ Traversal abilities (re-gated to story progression)

The mode is designed not just as a difficulty bump but as a **build experimentation sandbox** — NG+ lets you try weapon/ability combinations that weren't possible in a first playthrough because you couldn't unlock everything at once.

## Difficulty vs. Game Length: Does It Affect Playtime?

Yes, but less than you'd think. Based on [how long Control Resonant takes to beat](/games/control-resonant/how-long-to-beat):

| Setting | Estimated main story time |
|---|---|
| Story / assists on | ~25–28 hours (fewer retries) |
| Default difficulty | ~30 hours (the official figure) |
| Highest difficulty, no assists | ~35–40 hours (more deaths, more retries) |

Difficulty mostly affects **retry time**, not content volume. The 30-hour baseline from Mokhov assumes a competent player on default difficulty.

## FAQ: Difficulty Settings

### Can I change difficulty mid-playthrough?

Almost certainly yes. *Control (2019)* allowed difficulty changes at any time from the settings menu with no penalty, and the PS5 Store's "Adjustable Difficulty (Advanced)" classification implies the same flexibility. You won't be locked into a choice made at the start.

### Is there a permadeath mode?

Not confirmed. Remedy hasn't mentioned one, and the game's structure (open-ended zones, optional encounters, NG+) doesn't suggest a roguelike-style permadeath loop. If one exists, it would likely be a post-game unlock.

### Do assists disable achievements?

No. Based on *Control (2019)*'s policy — which explicitly allowed Assist Mode without locking any achievements or trophies — Remedy treats accessibility as a player choice, not a handicap. Expect the same in Control Resonant.

### What's the hardest difficulty called?

Not yet confirmed. Remedy may use named tiers (like *Control*'s lack of explicit names) or may introduce labels closer to launch. Watch for an announcement in the final weeks before the [September 24 release](/games/control-resonant/release-date).

### Is Control Resonant harder than Control?

Judging by the *Sekiro* and *DOOM* references from the creative director, **yes — at the top end**. The combat is faster, enemies have more agency (jumping, flying, varied sizes), and the Aberrant weapon's melee-first design demands more active engagement than *Control*'s gun-and-Launch combo. But the assist system means the floor is also lower — you can make it as easy as you need.

### Should I play on hard for my first run?

Only if you're confident in action-RPG combat and familiar with Remedy's pace. For everyone else, **default difficulty on a first run is the sweet spot** — you can always bump it up or start NG+ on hard if the default feels too easy.

## The Bottom Line

Control Resonant's difficulty system is built on a simple principle: **the game should meet you where you are.** Whether you're here for the story, the lore, the combat challenge, or the build-crafting, the settings let you shape the experience.

- **Story-first?** Turn on assists, drop difficulty, enjoy the ride.
- **Balanced?** Default difficulty, no assists, adjust if needed.
- **Challenge seeker?** Max difficulty, no assists, save NG+ for the real test.

Whatever you pick, the [Aberrant weapon's five forms](/games/control-resonant/aberrant-weapon-forms) and Dylan's paranatural abilities give you plenty of tools to work with.
