## What Reviewers Agree On (The Consensus)

Across the 84 average, **four pillars are nearly universal**:

1. **Art direction and world design is generation-defining.** VGC, GameSpot, IGN, Push Square, Eurogamer, and Polygon all lead with it — even the harshest reviews concede this is one of the best-looking games of the year. "Trailers have hinted at some of the arresting art design Resonant features, but it's truly something special to behold" (GameSpot).
2. **Boss encounters are standout.** The Resonant cycle bosses — multi-phase set pieces built around environmental shifts — are the single highest-praised combat element. "Both the world and the enemies, especially the bosses, are nothing short of jaw-dropping" (GameSpot).
3. **Accessibility options earn across-the-board praise.** The one-hit kills toggle, difficulty sliders, and assist modes drew compliments even from critical reviews — a rarity for hardcore-leaning outlets.
4. **Zoe de Vera and Dylan/Zoe chemistry.** GameSpot named Remedy's *Control* newcomer as a personal favorite, calling her the relationship that "actually drives the story's appeal" rather than the paranatural spectacle.

Two secondary consensus points: **storytelling is more conventional than usual for Remedy** (even positive reviewers note this), and **length lands around 25–30 hours for the main story** — Insider Gaming and GamerHub confirm credits roll around 25 hours, with completionist runs running significantly longer.

## What Divides Critics (The Real Story)

The 92% positive average hides a **sharply polarized reception** under the hood. Three fault lines split reviewers:

### 1. The Melee Combat Question

The single biggest divide. Positive outlets call it "responsive stylish-action foundationally sound" (IGN) with "fluid weapon-form swaps" (VGC). Negative outlets call it "bafflingly dull," "no parry," "no real combo system," and "just mashing, mashing, mashing" (ComicBook). The DMC / Bayonetta / God of War comparison is where this lands hardest — for action-game traditionalists, Resonant **doesn't give you enough tools to express yourself**.

### 2. The Open-Zone Pivot

Original *Control* was a Metroidvania-style single building (the Oldest House). Resonant moves to **open zones** across Manhattan. Eurogamer is the sharpest critic here: "Remedy rebukes the idea of Control Resonant being an open-world game, and it's not quite as freewheeling… but Resonant feels like it's chasing the hallmarks of a live-service game." Polygon agrees on the "padding out its open world with rote activities" critique. Positive outlets (VGC, GameSpot) read the same zones as "unlike anything I've ever seen" — same game, same zones, radically different interpretation.

### 3. Dylan vs. Jesse as Protagonist

Reviewers who clicked with Dylan call him "a real sad sack who feels sorry for himself — tragic and easy to root for" (IGN). Reviewers who didn't call him "a black hole of charisma" and "not done justice replacing Jesse as the lead" (ComicBook, Polygon). The split isn't about Dylan's writing quality — it's about whether **the player wanted to follow a new protagonist at all** after seven years of attachment to Jesse Faden.

**What this means for buyers:** the consensus is "great world, great bosses, divisive combat." If you loved *Control*'s tight single-building design and Jesse's protagonist voice, you're in the "wait and see" zone. If you wanted *Control* to expand into something more ambitious and traversal-focused, you're in the "pre-order now" zone.

## What's Changed Since Reviews (Launch-Day Snapshot)

Reviews went up on September 18, and we've been tracking what has shifted in the week between embargo lift and launch. Here's the picture roughly 24 hours after the global unlock — standard edition live worldwide since September 24, 7:00 AM PT:

### The Day-One Patch Notes Are Now Public — and They Answer the Reviews

At launch, Remedy published the day-one patch details, and they read as a **direct response to the review criticism**. The studio acknowledged "Dylan's overall combat strength early on in the game not feeling powerful enough (even if the man just woke up from a coma)" — the exact early-game difficulty spike that RPG Site and other reviewers flagged — and shipped a combat rebalance alongside the launch. Per Remedy: "We strongly believe these changes will improve the gameplay feel and flow while still providing a challenge."

What the patch actually changes:

- **Enemy health and stun windows reduced** across most enemy types — including the **Dancer** and **Deserter** boss fights, two of the early Resonant encounters reviewers called overtuned.
- **Maximum Aberrant and Ability strengths buffed** — Dylan's damage ceiling goes up, so late-game builds hit harder.
- **Respec made easier**: the stock of **Reset Items increased across the game's vendors**, so experimenting with builds is cheaper than at review.
- **UI updates** — the same QoL bucket as *Alan Wake 2*'s first-week patches.

The practical upshot: if you buy the game today, the difficulty curve that reviewers described in their September 18 copy is **already softer and better-balanced**. The PS5 Deluxe early-access players (who started September 22) have been on post-patch code from the start, and the "rough first hours" critique in several reviews no longer matches the shipped build.

### The Steam Pre-Load Re-Download Incident

One genuine launch-day stumble: the patch dropped on Steam roughly an hour before the unlock, and **it triggered a full re-download of the ~92 GB game for players who had pre-loaded**. Remedy apologized publicly on X: "This triggered certain platform mechanisms on PC, forcing players who had already downloaded the game to download it again, thereby causing you unnecessary inconvenience. This was an oversight on our part. We don't have an excuse."

If you pre-loaded on Steam, the patch already applied and you're on the final build — the re-download only affected the pre-launch window. New buyers download once.

### First 24 Hours of Full Launch: What Players Are Saying

With the standard edition live across all timezones, the early player verdict is taking shape:

- **Steam early rating: ~80% Positive** (100+ reviews in the first 24 hours) — a solid early score for a demanding action game, and notably in line with the critic consensus rather than rebelling against it.
- **What players praise**: the atmosphere, story, and Remedy-style presentation; combat described as fluid and responsive; sound design and visuals drawing frequent mentions — the same "world first" consensus the critics reached.
- **The main complaint: PC performance variance.** The recurring negative reviews cite **stuttering, frame-time instability, and setup-dependent performance** — smooth on many modern rigs, problematic on others. This matches the review-era PC caveat (top-end hardware benchmarks fine; mid-range and older builds are the risk zone).
- **No game-breaking bugs at scale.** No widespread progression-blocker or crash reports have surfaced across platforms in the first day of full launch — the same pattern the 36-hour PS5 Deluxe early-access window showed.

### First 36 Hours of Player Feedback (PS5 Deluxe Early Access)

The PS5 Deluxe 48-hour early access opened on September 22 at 7:00 AM PT. By launch day morning, tens of thousands of players had put in serious time on the post-patch build. The pattern from launch-day hands-on coverage and the emerging player reports:

- **Overall polish is strong.** Rectify Gaming called the post-patch build "exceptionally well" performing, with "smooth frame rates, fast loading times, and no bugs" on the base PS5 hardware they tested. The technical consensus is that Remedy shipped Resonant in a clean state.
- **Two specific base PS5 performance notes to know.** Game8's base PS5 review confirmed **Performance Mode targets 60fps but can dip into the sub-50s during heavier Resonant boss encounters** (one specific example cited was a boss that spews lava across the arena), and that there are **stutter moments during fast traversal** as the world streams in. These are NOT launch-blockers, but they're real base PS5 performance edges worth knowing if you're playing on a stock console.
- **Lock-on camera has a minor quirk.** Game8 reported that lock-on can briefly drift to off-screen enemies during combos, breaking combat flow. The simple workaround is to toggle off lock-on until Remedy ships a follow-up patch. This is in the same minor-QoL bucket as *Alan Wake 2*'s first-week patches.
- **No game-breaking crashes reported.** No widespread crash-to-desktop or progression-blocker reports have surfaced in the first 36 hours of early-access play across major platforms.

For reference, the early-access window's verdict held up: PS5 Deluxe players stress-tested the post-patch build for 36+ hours before anyone else got in, and their reports set the pattern the full-launch player base is now confirming.

## Why the Timing Was Good News (And What the Scores Confirm)

Six days between reviews and launch is on the generous end for a single-player AAA. The comparison worth making:

- Publishers confident in quality lift embargoes **4–7 days out** — they want positive word-of-mouth compounding into launch week.
- Publishers nervous about reception hold reviews to **24–48 hours before launch** (or launch day itself) — enough time for headlines, not enough for deliberation.

Remedy choosing September 18 for a September 24 ship — with the announcement made a full three days before the lift — read as a studio that **knew the scores would land in the "solid B-tier, not universal praise" zone**. ## What Reviews Will Tell You (and What They Won't)

Now that scores are in, the buying decision is more concrete:

**Reviews ARE useful for:**
1. **Performance**: which platform runs best? Is the PC version optimized, or does it need patches? (The RTX 5070 Ti 1440p numbers above are your best day-one signal for PC.)
2. **Combat depth**: does the shape-shifting Aberrant combat deliver on the DMC / Bayonetta inspirations Remedy has cited? **Mixed verdict** — see the "What Divides Critics" section above.
3. **Content volume**: main story length (25–30 hours per our [playtime guide](/games/control-resonant/how-long-to-beat)), side content quality, and whether the open-world Manhattan structure holds up. **Largely positive** on world, mixed on padding.

**Reviews WON'T tell you:**
- **Your own combat preference.** Difficulty is [fully adjustable](/games/control-resonant/difficulty-settings) with one-hit kills assist, so a reviewer who disliked the challenge may not reflect your experience.
- **How much you value the protagonist change.** If you've been waiting seven years for Jesse's story to continue, only you know how you'll feel about Dylan taking the lead.

The "Day-One Patch Notes Are Now Public" section above has the details. One PC caveat: if you're on mid-range or older hardware, the stutter reports in the Steam reviews are worth a skim before you commit.

The bottom-line decision is no longer about timing — reviews are public and the day-one patch is shipped. It's now about **whether the reviews' mixed verdict matches your taste**.
