Every piece of Control Resonant marketing has one visual at its center: **the Aberrant**, the shapeshifting weapon Dylan Faden wields across a warped Manhattan. It's the sequel's answer to Jesse Faden's Service Weapon — but where the Service Weapon was a gun that changed form, the Aberrant is a melee weapon whose combat identity you build yourself.

This guide ranks all six Aberrant forms, breaks down the primary/secondary loadout system, and names the best form combos for every kind of fight — based on the full post-launch kit, not pre-launch guesses.

> **Correction (Oct 4):** An earlier version of this page described a pre-launch "5 forms" system (Dual Blades / Axe / Scythe / Whip / Heavy Hammer). That was speculation that didn't survive launch. The shipped game has **6 forms in a primary + secondary loadout system**, detailed below. If you read the old version, disregard it — this page now matches the actual game.

## How the Aberrant Actually Works

The Aberrant is a **two-slot weapon system**, not a morph-on-the-fly palette:

- **Primary forms (3):** Strike, Glaive, Cleave — your main attack moveset
- **Secondary forms (3):** Crush, Extend, Drill — a distinct offhand attack you can weave in alongside your primary
- You equip **one primary + one secondary at a time** in your Loadout, and swap the pairing at will
- Each pairing also unlocks a **Combo Finisher** — there are six of them across the possible form combinations

New forms are unlocked with **Living Shards** (three for a primary form, two for a secondary), and upgraded inside **The Gap** — the same hub where Dylan's resonance abilities live. Your weapon build and your ability build are two halves of the same decision tree.

## Primary Forms, Ranked

### S — Strike

**Playstyle:** Strike is the fastest moveset in the game — rapid chains against a single target, with crit scaling that rewards landing the full string. It's the form the combat engine is most tightly tuned around: hit volume feeds your sustain loop (kills heal you in Resonant — there are no healing items), and nothing produces hits like Strike.

**Strengths:**
- Highest hits-per-second of any primary — fastest way to build and cash out on-window damage
- Crit-fishing strings melt bosses once you learn their tells
- Short recovery means a whiffed string costs you almost nothing

**Weaknesses:**
- Single-target focused — swarms require your secondary to cover you
- Demands consistent dodge timing, since you live inside melee range

**When to use it:** Bosses, elites, and any duel where the only thing that matters is damage output.

### A — Cleave

**Playstyle:** The balanced primary. Cleave doesn't top any single stat, but its attack strings are the most forgiving in the kit, and its backstab bonus means smart repositioning is directly rewarded. For most of the campaign, Cleave is the form you can forget to think about — in the best way.

**Strengths:**
- Forgiving recovery on every attack
- Backstab damage bonus rewards dodge-through repositioning
- Works in every chapter of the game without feeling outclassed

**Weaknesses:**
- No specialty — Strike out-damages it on single targets, Glaive out-clears it on crowds

**When to use it:** General play, unfamiliar encounters, and any fight where you're still learning the enemy's moveset.

### B — Glaive

**Playstyle:** The crowd-control primary. Glaive's wide sweeps hit everything in an arc, making it the answer to the swarming thralls that pack warped Manhattan. Its weakness is focus: when a boss is the only thing on screen, Glaive's wide arcs waste most of their potential.

**Strengths:**
- Best crowd clear in the weapon
- Wide arcs make whiffs nearly impossible
- Strong in the pack-heavy street and subway encounters

**Weaknesses:**
- Weakest primary against single bosses
- Slower strings mean less crit fishing than Strike

**When to use it:** Zones with heavy mob density, and as the "safe" pick when you're under-leveled for an area.

## Secondary Forms, Ranked

### S — Crush

**Playstyle:** The heavy slam. Crush has the highest **Falter** application in the game — Falter being the stagger state that opens enemies to finishers and takedowns. A well-timed Crush also knocks lighter enemies flat, buying you free seconds in the middle of a pack.

**Strengths:**
- Highest Falter per hit — the finisher-loop's best friend
- Knockdowns create breathing room
- Excellent armor-break against elites

**Weaknesses:**
- Slow windup — the most punishable attack if you mistime it
- Short reach

**When to use it:** Against everything with a Falter bar you actually want to break: elites, armored units, bosses.

### A — Extend

**Playstyle:** The whip. Extend turns your loadout into a mid-range tool — pokes, sweeping arcs, and pulls that yank enemies out of formation. It's the only secondary that lets you fight while *not* standing inside the danger zone.

**Strengths:**
- Longest reach of any form
- Pull mechanics isolate priority targets
- Interrupts enemy attacks from outside their range

**Weaknesses:**
- Lowest sustained damage of the secondaries
- Precision-dependent

**When to use it:** Learning a new enemy's moveset, fighting ranged-heavy packs, and any fight where you need to control spacing.

### B — Drill

**Playstyle:** The grinder. Drill's sustained piercing attacks apply **Weakened** — a debuff that amplifies the damage enemies take from everything, including your primary form. It's a set-up tool: drill first, then cash out with Strike or Crush.

**Strengths:**
- Applies Weakened, multiplying your total damage output
- Sustained piercing handles armored units well
- Synergizes with every primary in the game

**Weaknesses:**
- Feels weak in isolation — its value is entirely in the debuff
- Demands commitment to short range for the full pierce duration

**When to use it:** Long fights against big health pools — bosses, Resonant-tier enemies, and anything you intend to kill with a finisher.

## Combo Finishers: The Real Skill Ceiling

Each primary/secondary pairing grants a distinct **Combo Finisher** — the six named finishers in the shipped game are **Concussive Beatdown, Uppercut Surge, Wide Edge, Spin Edge, Split Pivot, and Split Orbit**. They're your highest-damage windows and the payoff for all the Falter-building and Weakened-applying the forms do.

The practical takeaway: your *form pairing* determines your finisher, so the "best weapon in Control Resonant" question is really "best pairing" — pick the primary you fight with, and the secondary whose debuff/falter profile feeds the finisher you want to land.

## FAQ

### How many weapon forms are in Control Resonant?
**Six** — three primary forms (Strike, Glaive, Cleave) and three secondary forms (Crush, Extend, Drill). You equip one of each simultaneously in your Loadout.

### What is the best weapon in Control Resonant?
**Strike + Crush** is the strongest overall pairing for bosses and elites. For general campaign play, **Cleave + Extend** is the most forgiving and versatile combination.

### How do I switch Aberrant forms?
Open your Loadout and swap your primary/secondary pairing. Forms are unlocked with Living Shards (three for a primary, two for a secondary) and upgraded in The Gap.

### Is the Aberrant a gun like the Service Weapon?
No. The Service Weapon from Control was a shapeshifting *gun*. The Aberrant is a shapeshifting *melee* weapon built from six forms in a primary + secondary loadout system.

### Do the forms level up separately?
Forms are upgraded individually inside The Gap, and your [skill tree](/games/control-resonant/skill-tree) interacts with your equipped pairing — progress on a form is kept even when you swap it out of your Loadout.

## The Bottom Line

The Aberrant is Control Resonant's most important system — your weapon, your build, and your playstyle in one morphing package. Strike duels, Glaive clears, Cleave adapts; Crush breaks, Extend controls, Drill sets up. The real skill ceiling isn't any single form — it's choosing the pairing the moment demands and landing the finisher it unlocks.
