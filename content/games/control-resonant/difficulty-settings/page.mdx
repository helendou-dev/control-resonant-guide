*Last updated: September 25, 2026 — verified against the retail build and the day-one patch.*

**Short answer: there is no Easy / Normal / Hard menu in Control Resonant. One default difficulty ships with the game, and every bit of tuning lives inside Assist Mode — four presets (Field Agent, Strategist, Ranger, Custom) plus eleven individual settings you can push in either direction.** Open it from **Options → Gameplay → Assist Mode** while you're in a save, and change it as often as you like. Nothing is locked out for using it — not trophies, not achievements, not endings.

That last part matters more than it sounds. This is the most flexible difficulty system Remedy has shipped, and it works backwards too: the same sliders that make the game easier can make it considerably harder than any preset.

## How Difficulty Actually Works

Forget what you'd expect from a settings menu. In Control Resonant:

- **There is no difficulty select screen.** Load into a save first — Assist Mode lives inside the in-game options, not the main menu.
- **The system is per save slot.** Different playthroughs can carry completely different setups.
- **You can toggle it on or off mid-fight, mid-mission, mid-anything.** No reload, no penalty, no "cheater" flag.
- **It warns you first.** The game notes that the default difficulty is the intended experience — take that as a suggestion, not a rule. Remedy's lead gameplay designer Sergey Mohov put the studio's position plainly: *"if you run into a wall and the friction becomes too much, we'd much rather you adjust the rules and keep playing than put the controller down and stop."*

That's the whole design philosophy in one sentence, and it's worth taking at face value: the studio would rather you finish the game than prove something to it.

## The Four Presets (and What They Actually Do)

Presets are starting points, not difficulty tiers — the moment you touch an individual setting, the game switches you to Custom.

| Preset | Official description | Feels like |
|---|---|---|
| **Custom** | Any manual mix of settings. Selected automatically when you change an individual option. | Whatever you built |
| **Field Agent** | Brings story and exploration to the forefront, combat rarely putting you in danger. | Story mode |
| **Strategist** | Keeps fights fast and lethal for both sides, rewarding precise play where the slightest mistake matters. | The closest thing to "normal" |
| **Ranger** | Lets you stand your ground and shrug off attacks, trading firepower for staying power. | Longer, scrappier fights — lower damage both ways |

The trap most players fall into is grabbing a preset on hour one. Remedy's own recommendation is the opposite: play the intended combat rhythm first, then adjust **the one mechanic that's actually blocking you** — not the whole profile.

## All 11 Settings Explained

Read this list with an eye for the direction column. Half of these make the game harder when turned up.

| Setting | What it changes | Turn it up to… |
|---|---|---|
| **Enemy Aggression** | How many enemies attack you at once (low / medium / high) | Get swarmed more — real added pressure |
| **Enemy Projectile Speed** | How fast ranged attacks travel | Make dodging harder |
| **Perfect Dodge Window** | Timing tolerance for a Perfect Dodge | Punish your timing — *reduce* this to make it harder |
| **Incoming Damage** | Damage Dylan takes | Die faster |
| **Outgoing Damage** | Damage Dylan deals | End fights quicker |
| **Outgoing Falter** | How fast you break enemy posture (the bar under their health) | Create more execution openings |
| **Power Recovery Speed** | How fast Dylan's Power refills for abilities | Use abilities far more often — *reduce* to starve yourself |
| **Enhanced Melee Magnetizing** | Auto-closes distance on melee targets | — (toggle; fixes whiffed swings and camera-range problems) |
| **Immortality** | Dylan still takes damage but can't die | — (toggle) |
| **One-Hit Kills** | Enemies — including bosses — die in a single hit | — (toggle) |
| **One-Hit Falters** | Instantly fills an enemy's Falter bar, enabling an instant execution | — (toggle) |

The last three are the nuclear options. Useful for clearing a mission you want to get past, or for guide writers — less useful if you actually want the combat.

## Which Setup Fits Your Playstyle

### If you're here for the story
**Field Agent**, and only escalate if a specific fight stops you. If one boss is the wall, don't rebuild the whole profile — drop **Incoming Damage** a notch or widen the **Perfect Dodge Window**. That's usually enough.

### If you want the intended experience
Stay on default for the first few hours, then judge. Just know that "default" is front-loaded: early Dylan has barely any abilities, so the opening is the hardest the game ever is. Most reviewers describe a curve where the game gets *easier* by hour ten, not harder. If you're getting flattened at the start, that's the design, not your failure — the fix is time, not pride.

### If you're getting overwhelmed (not out-damaged)
Crowd pressure is a different problem from damage, and it has a different fix:

- **Enemy Aggression → low or medium** so fewer enemies commit at once and you can read the arena
- **Enemy Projectile Speed down into the 80s** if ranged spam is what's actually killing you
- **Enhanced Melee Magnetizing on** if swings keep missing

Don't immediately reach for damage numbers. Reducing pressure changes the pace; reducing damage just removes the need to fight well.

### If fights are taking too long
That's an offence problem, not a defence one. Raise **Outgoing Damage** (moderate steps are plenty — you want shorter fights, not absent ones) and **Outgoing Falter** so you hit execution windows more often. Combine that with the [Aberrant's faster forms](/games/control-resonant/aberrant-weapon-forms) and combat stops dragging.

### If abilities feel too rare
Raise **Power Recovery Speed**. The combat loop runs on Power — you charge it with melee hits and spend it on abilities — so this single slider changes how much of your toolkit is available per fight. Full breakdown in our [combat systems guide](/games/control-resonant/combat-systems-preview).

### If you want it harder than any preset
You can build something more demanding than Strategist ever gets: raise **Enemy Aggression** to high, speed up **Enemy Projectile Speed**, shrink the **Perfect Dodge Window**, and cut **Power Recovery Speed**. Remedy kept the harder direction open on purpose — it's how you get a challenge run without a separate mode.

## What the Day-One Patch Changed About Difficulty

This is the part most launch-day guides haven't caught up with. Remedy shipped a patch on launch and admitted, in its own words, that Dylan's combat strength "early on in the game" wasn't feeling powerful enough — which matched exactly what reviewers had flagged as the game's weak spot.

What the patch did:

- **Lowered enemy base HP and tightened stagger windows**, including on **Dancer** and **Deserter**, two of the Resonant bosses
- **Raised the maximum strength of the Aberrant and abilities**, improving your ceiling later in the run
- Adjusted **Reset Items** (respec consumables) stock at vendors, plus UI touch-ups

Translation: if you read a review complaining about spongy early fights, that criticism was partly addressed before most players ever booted the game. You shouldn't need to open Assist Mode in the first hour just to survive — the [Resonant Cycle](/games/control-resonant/everything-we-know-so-far) curve does most of that work for you as abilities accumulate.

## Does Using Assists Block Trophies?

No. Assist Mode doesn't gate trophies or achievements in any configuration — including Immortality and One-Hit Kills. Control Resonant ships with **50 trophies (1 Platinum / 1 Gold / 16 Silver / 32 Bronze)** and none of them are difficulty-locked.

That's consistent with how Remedy treated the first Control's Assist Mode, and it means a full 100% run is achievable entirely on your own terms.

## New Game+ Difficulty

New Game+ has been confirmed since before launch, and it raises the baseline rather than repeating the campaign. What carries over:

- ✅ Aberrant form mastery and upgrades
- ✅ Paranatural abilities and skill-tree investments
- ✅ Artifacts and collected trinkets
- ❌ Traversal abilities (re-gated to story progression)

NG+ is built as a build-experimentation sandbox as much as a difficulty bump — it's where you finally get to run combinations a single playthrough can't unlock at once. Expect to reach it around [the 30-hour mark](/games/control-resonant/how-long-to-beat).

## Accessibility Beyond Difficulty

Difficulty is one layer of a much wider stack — subtitle sizing, colour palettes, motion and lens-effect toggles, per-category volume, full input remapping. See the dedicated [accessibility and Assist Mode guide](/games/control-resonant/accessibility-options) for the complete list.

## FAQ: Difficulty Settings

### Does Control Resonant have difficulty settings?

Not in the traditional sense. There's no Easy / Normal / Hard ladder — one default difficulty ships with the game and everything else is Assist Mode.

### Can I change difficulty mid-playthrough?

Yes, at any point, from Options → Gameplay → Assist Mode. The setting is tied to your save slot, so two playthroughs can run completely different configurations.

### Do assists disable achievements?

No. Trophies and achievements unlock regardless of Assist Mode, including at the most extreme settings.

### Can Assist Mode make the game harder?

Yes — Enemy Aggression, Enemy Projectile Speed, Perfect Dodge Window and Power Recovery Speed can all be set against you, producing a run harder than any preset.

### What's the easiest way to play?

**Field Agent**, or manually switch on **Immortality** and **One-Hit Kills** if you purely want the story and the world. Neither costs you a trophy.

### Is Control Resonant harder than Control?

At the top end, yes — the melee pivot means more active engagement and more threats from more directions, and there's no parry to fall back on. But the floor is also lower than the original's was at launch, and the day-one patch softened the punishing opening. The gap between "hardest possible" and "easiest possible" here is wider than in any Remedy game so far.

### Should I start on the highest available difficulty?

Only with experience in action-RPG combat and Remedy's pacing. For a first run, default-and-adjust is the better call: you keep the intended rhythm, and you can always shift the sliders or save the real test for NG+.

## The Bottom Line

Control Resonant's difficulty system makes one bet: that you'd rather keep playing than be filtered out. It's why there's no preset menu to agonise over, why the sliders run both directions, and why nothing is gated behind them.

- **Story-first:** Field Agent, adjust one slider at a time.
- **Intended experience:** default difficulty, judge it after the opening hours — the early game is the hardest part of the curve.
- **More pressure:** crank aggression and projectile speed, shrink the dodge window.
- **Just want past a fight:** Immortality and One-Hit Kills, then turn them off. No penalty either way.

Whatever you land on, the [five Aberrant forms](/games/control-resonant/aberrant-weapon-forms) and Dylan's abilities give you the tools. If you're just starting, the [beginner guide](/games/control-resonant/beginner-guide) covers what to do in your first few hours — including the mistakes that make the opening feel harder than it is.
