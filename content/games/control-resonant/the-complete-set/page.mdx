**Short answer: The Complete Set is a five-speaker collection quest. Four SINKKI D50 speakers are scattered across Downtown, Central, and the West Incursion Zone — each one possessed, bouncing around, and only containable in a still moment. The fifth, the subwoofer, sits in an Oldest House storage room behind an SR-level keycard Langston gives you. Bring all five to the Auction House ritual, follow the five-surface rule (glass, stone, wood, blue tarp — and the subwoofer must touch water), and you'll walk away with the Untapped Bout Guard artifact no matter which ending you pick.**

## How the Quest Works

The Complete Set is easy to miss and easy to misunderstand, because the game explains almost none of it. The premise: a complete vintage SINKKI D50 speaker set is scattered across Manhattan, every piece of it is paranaturally misbehaving, and an auction house buyer wants the full set staged for a ritual purchase.

The rules:

- **The quest has two natural starting points** — most players trip over it at the Museum of Curiosities in the West Incursion Zone, where a speaker is part of the exhibit, or at the Auction House in Downtown, where the buyer's ritual is set up and waiting.
- **Speakers can only be contained while still.** A possessed D50 hops and slides around its space on its own schedule. Wait for the pause between jolts, then interact — chasing it mid-bounce does nothing.
- **The subwoofer is gated.** Unlike the four regular speakers, it's locked in Oldest House storage behind an SR-level door, and the keycard comes from a person, not a chest.
- **The ritual has a fixed five-surface rule**, and the game never states it outright — see the Auction House section for the full order.
- **Your final choice doesn't change your reward.** It changes the scene and one NPC's fate, not your loot.

You can collect the four district speakers in any order. The subwoofer detour is worth doing whenever you're next in the Oldest House — there's no story gate beyond the keycard conversation.

## Speaker 1: The Auction House (Downtown)

The Auction House is the quest's stage and most players' first stop — the ritual circle of surfaces is already laid out here (you'll use it later). The first D50 is loose on the main floor. It jolts in short hops, freezes for a beat, and jolts again. **Contain it during the freeze.** If you interact while it's moving, nothing happens — back off, wait for the next pause, try again.

## Speaker 2: The Construction Site (Central)

Central's construction site speaker is the most mobile of the four: it bounces across scaffolding, rubble, and equipment. The containment logic is identical — it can't be caught mid-air. Corner it on flat ground, let it settle, and grab it in the gap. Clearing nearby Hiss first helps; combat respawns bounce it back into motion.

## Speaker 3: Atlantis Café (Central)

The coffee-shop speaker skitters between the tables and behind the counter. It's the least mobile of the set and usually the fastest contain: follow it, wait out two or three hops, interact during the pause. While you're in Central, this pairs naturally with the district's [secret hideout](/games/control-resonant/secret-hideouts) — the Laundromat puzzle is in the same zone.

## Speaker 4: Museum of Curiosities (West Incursion Zone)

The museum speaker sits on a plinth as a seemingly ordinary exhibit — until you approach and it hops off. This is where most players first realize something is *wrong* with the entire SINKKI D50 line, which is the quest's best bit of environmental storytelling. Contain it the same way: let it finish its escape attempt, interact during the still moment.

## Speaker 5: The Subwoofer — Oldest House Storage

The subwoofer is the quest's real gate, and it works differently from the other four:

1. **Talk to Langston.** The SR-level keycard you need is a conversation reward — Langston hands it over for the retrieval. If the storage door reads as locked and you have no keycard, you haven't had this conversation yet.
2. **Head to the Oldest House storage area near Executive Research.** The door is SR-level; the keycard opens it.
3. **Opening the room triggers a Hiss fight.** Clear it first — the subwoofer is inert until the room is quiet, and it cannot be contained with hostiles active.

Once the room is clear, the subwoofer contains like the others. This is the only speaker with a combat requirement and the only one that costs a detour — but it's also the piece the ritual literally cannot work without.

## The Auction House Ritual: The Five-Surface Rule

With all five pieces, return to the Auction House. The ritual space is defined by **five surfaces**, and the order matters:

| Surface | What goes on it |
|---|---|
| **Glass** | A regular speaker |
| **Stone** | A regular speaker |
| **Wood** | A regular speaker |
| **Blue tarp** | A regular speaker |
| **Water** | The subwoofer — it must touch the water |

Three of the four regular speakers slot onto any of the solid surfaces; the hard rules are that all four go up, and **the subwoofer goes in the water**. The game signals none of this — if the ritual won't complete, it's almost always a mis-placed subwoofer.

Then the finale: the set powers on, the ritual peaks, and **four of the five speakers start pulsing**. Your job is to **turn off the four regular speakers one by one during the gaps between pulses, leaving the subwoofer for last.** Interacting during an active pulse fails; wait for the beat of silence between them.

## The Choice: Turn It Off or Turn It Up

With only the subwoofer left, the game hands you a binary with zero guidance:

| Option | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Turn the subwoofer off** | The ritual collapses. The Gate stays closed. The civilian tied up in the ritual **dies**. |
| **Turn the volume up** | The ritual completes. **The Gate opens.** The civilian survives — possessed by multiple entities, which the game frames as a different, arguably worse fate. |

There is no third option, no hidden "best" answer, and — the part most players want to know — **no difference in your reward.** Either way the quest completes and you receive the **Untapped Bout Guard** artifact. Pick whichever ending fits your Dylan's conscience; completionists lose nothing either way.

## The Reward: Untapped Bout Guard

The Bout Guard is a build-defining artifact for ability-heavy playstyles:

- **Power recovery +50%** — your ability fuel refills half again as fast
- **Incoming damage +20%** — you take a fifth more damage from everything

It's a pure glass-cannon trade: more uptime on your Resonance abilities, less survivability while using them. That pairs naturally with builds that already lean on the [skill tree's](/games/control-resonant/skill-tree) ability branches, and it's *especially* potent in NG++ loops where the Power Core Charge Duration slider (added in [patch 1.4](/games/control-resonant/new-game-plus)) stacks charge duration with recovery speed. If your build is melee-first and rarely casts, the trade is much worse — see the [difficulty and Assist Mode guide](/games/control-resonant/difficulty-settings) for tuning incoming damage back down (the Incoming Damage slider works regardless of what artifacts you slot).

## Known Issue: The Final Prompt May Not Appear

As of patch 1.4, multiple players report that the **final interaction prompt — the one that confirms your choice on the subwoofer — occasionally fails to appear**, leaving the ritual hanging with no way to proceed.

Workarounds that players report fixing it:

- **Leave the Auction House entirely, re-enter, and re-approach** the subwoofer — the prompt re-registers on a fresh load of the space.
- **Reload from the last checkpoint** if the space re-entry doesn't do it. The quest state (speakers delivered) persists; only the prompt is lost.

If Remedy patches this in a future update, we'll remove the section — until then, don't assume your save is bricked.

## FAQ

**How many SINKKI D50 speakers are there?**
Five — four regular speakers (Auction House in Downtown, construction site and Atlantis Café in Central, Museum of Curiosities in the West Incursion Zone) plus one subwoofer in Oldest House storage.

**How do I contain a speaker that keeps moving?**
Wait for the pause. Possessed speakers jolt in bursts with still moments between them, and containment only works during the still moments. Chasing or interacting mid-hop does nothing.

**Where is the SR-level keycard?**
Langston gives it to you for the subwoofer retrieval — it's a conversation reward, not a pickup. No keycard means you missed the talk; the storage door near Executive Research stays locked until you've had it.

**Does the subwoofer have to go in the water?**
Yes. The ritual's five-surface rule requires the four regular speakers up on their surfaces and the subwoofer touching water. A dry subwoofer is the most common reason the ritual won't complete.

**Which choice is canon / better — turn it off or turn it up?**
Neither affects your reward; you get the Untapped Bout Guard either way. The difference is the scene: turning it off closes the Gate and kills the civilian; turning it up opens the Gate and leaves the civilian alive but possessed. There's no achievement difference either.

**Is the Untapped Bout Guard worth using?**
For ability-heavy builds, absolutely — +50% power recovery is one of the strongest uptime stats in the game. For melee builds, the +20% incoming damage makes it a poor trade unless you compensate with the Assist Mode Incoming Damage slider.

**Is The Complete Set missable?**
Launch-week guides don't report any point of no return — the speakers stay where they are through the story, and free roam continues after the credits. The bigger risk is simply not finding the quest; it starts from either the Museum of Curiosities or the Auction House, whichever you hit first.

**Does NG++ reset The Complete Set?**
Yes — zones, secrets, and world events reset with each NG++ cycle on the same save file (patch 1.4, live October 1), which makes the quest's artifact and its scene choice replayable on every loop. See the [NG+ guide](/games/control-resonant/new-game-plus) for the full carryover picture.
