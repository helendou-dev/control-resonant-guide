Two things stand out immediately:

1. **"Adjustable Difficulty (Advanced)"** — Sony's "Advanced" tag is its most flexible accessibility classification. It signals a system well beyond a simple Easy / Normal / Hard picker: you get granular control, meaning you can tune difficulty *per element* rather than all-or-nothing.
2. **"A range of assists individually"** — the official wording. This is the direct confirmation that Control Resonant has an assist system, and that each assist can be toggled on or off independently rather than as one "Easy Mode" package.

## Assist Mode: What It Is and What It Includes

"Assist Mode" isn't a separate game mode in Control Resonant — it's the umbrella term for the individual assist toggles you can activate from the settings menu. Think of it as a toolbox rather than a difficulty preset.

### What *Control (2019)*'s Assist Mode Included

Remedy's previous game is the best evidence for what Resonant's assist system offers. *Control*'s Assist Mode let players independently adjust:

- **Damage dealt** multiplier (increase your output)
- **Damage taken** multiplier (reduce incoming damage, down to near-immortality)
- **Energy recovery** speed (for Launch and other powers)
- **Ammo replenishment** rate
- **Immortality toggle** (survive any combat hit)

Crucially, *Control* told players these tools carried **no penalty, no achievement lock, and no "cheater" flag**. The developers' stated philosophy: the story is for everyone, the challenge is for those who want it.

### What's Confirmed for Control Resonant

The gamescom media presentation (August 26, reported by 4Gamer) confirmed the assist system directly. Control Resonant's Assist Mode includes:

- **Presets by playstyle** — pick a starting template instead of tuning from scratch
- **Individually adjustable parameters**, including:
  - **Power recovery speed** — how fast your abilities recharge
  - **Damage taken** — raise or lower incoming damage
  - **Whether enemies' attacks can kill you** — a survival toggle, mirroring *Control*'s immortality option
- Values can be tuned **up or down** — the same system works to make the game *harder*, too

That's a near one-to-one evolution of *Control (2019)*'s Assist Mode, now with playstyle presets on top. One important design note from the same presentation: the game has **no traditional Easy/Normal/Hard ladder** — there's a single default difficulty, and all challenge tuning flows through the assist parameters. That's what "Adjustable Difficulty (Advanced)" on the store listing actually means.

### Possible additions we're still waiting to confirm

- **Parry/dodge timing assistance** — wider windows or auto-dodge options for the melee-heavy combat (the game has no guard or parry; dodging is the core defense)
- **Execution assistance** — easier [Gap openings](/games/control-resonant/combat-systems-preview) for players who can't land the timing

## Setting Up Control Resonant for Your Needs

You won't be locked into anything at launch — every option listed here is adjustable from the main menu, and the difficulty system itself is changeable mid-playthrough. Here's how to approach setup depending on what you need.

### For Story-First Players

If you're here for the narrative and lore (which is a *lot* of this game — see our [story recap](/games/control-resonant/story-recap)), the goal is frictionless play:

- Activate the assist toggles from the start — don't wait until you're frustrated
- Look for **damage taken reduction** and **immortality-style** assists first
- Keep **damage dealt** near default so combat still feels satisfying
- Use **game pausing** liberally — confirmed available during gameplay and cinematics (offline)

### For Players Who Need More Time to React

- Turn on **large subtitles** so you never miss dialogue while watching the action
- Reduce **stick sensitivity** for more precise camera control
- If you're on controller, check the **stick inversion** options for your preference
- Use the **individual volume controls** to balance voice (for dialogue-heavy moments) against SFX/music

### For Players With Visual or Hearing Needs

The confirmed audio suite is a strong start: **independent volume sliders** (mute, SFX, music, voice separately) plus **mono audio** — the latter is a real boon for players with hearing in only one ear or on one side of headphones. **Large subtitles** cover the dialogue side.

Remedy hasn't announced specific color-blind or contrast modes beyond what the store lists. *Control (2019)* had modest color-blind support, so we'd expect *some* iteration — but we won't speculate past what's confirmed until Remedy says more.

## Does Using Assists Hurt Anything?

**No.** This is the single most important thing to know if you're on the fence about using assist options:

- **No achievement/trophy lock** — *Control (2019)* explicitly allowed 100% completion with assists on; expect the same
- **No "easy mode" stigma** — the assists are individual toggles, not a labeled difficulty
- **No punishment** — you can turn assists on or off at any time mid-save

If you're still deciding *whether* the game is for you, our [difficulty settings guide](/games/control-resonant/difficulty-settings) has the full breakdown of what each setting does and which to pick per playstyle.

## Accessibility vs. Difficulty: Where They Overlap

One clarification that trips people up: in Control Resonant, **accessibility and difficulty are the same system.** The "Adjustable Difficulty" menu *is* where the assists live — there isn't a separate hidden accessibility screen. This is actually great news:

- One menu to learn
- Adjustable at any time
- No separate "easy mode" you have to feel weird about using

The classification "Adjustable Difficulty (Advanced)" from Sony covers both the difficulty presets and the assist toggles under one roof. Our [difficulty guide](/games/control-resonant/difficulty-settings) breaks down how the two interact.
