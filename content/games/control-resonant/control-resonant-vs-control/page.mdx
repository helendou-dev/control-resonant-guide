Control Resonant is the follow-up to 2019's *Control* — but calling it a straightforward sequel would be misleading. Remedy itself describes the two games as "siblings" rather than successor and predecessor, and nearly every pillar of the original has been rebuilt: the hero, the weapon, the setting, even the genre.

If you played *Control* and are wondering whether Resonant is more of the same, this side-by-side breaks down everything that's changed — and the few threads that carry over.

## Quick Comparison Table

| Feature | Control (2019) | Control Resonant (2026) |
|---------|----------------|-------------------------|
| Protagonist | Jesse Faden, new FBC Director | Dylan Faden, her brother |
| Genre | Action-adventure (third-person shooter) | Action-RPG |
| Primary weapon | Service Weapon (shape-shifting gun) | The Aberrant (shape-shifting melee weapon) |

| Structure | Metroidvania-style interconnected HQ | Extraction Zones + linear story missions |
| Progression | Mods and ability points | Skill trees per form, builds, artifacts, free respec |

## 1. You Play as Dylan, Not Jesse

The single biggest change: Jesse Faden is not the playable character. Control Resonant takes place roughly seven years after the first game, and you control **Dylan Faden** — Jesse's younger brother, who spent the original corrupted by the Hiss and ended the game in a coma.

In Resonant, Dylan wakes up. The FBC releases him for one mission: a paranatural entity has warped Manhattan, and Dylan is the only person capable of confronting it. Along the way he's searching for Jesse, who has "gone fishing" — a very Remedy euphemism for missing in action.

Creative director Mikael Kasurinen has said the sibling split was the plan from the start: "I always knew I wanted to first do a game about Jesse and then the second is about Dylan. Control is always about these siblings." Where Jesse was an outsider rising to become Director, Dylan is an insider the Bureau once imprisoned — a darker, more volatile perspective.

## 2. Melee Instead of Gunplay

If Jesse uses a gun, Dylan uses a melee weapon — that's the design logic straight from Kasurinen. The **Service Weapon**, Jesse's shape-shifting firearm, is replaced by the **Aberrant**, a crude, transforming melee weapon manifested by the Board.

The shift changes the entire feel of combat:

- **Control (2019)**: deliberate, ranged, cover-based shooting supplemented by telekinesis (Launch, Shield, Seize)
- **Control Resonant**: push-forward, aggressive, mobile — closer to DOOM's momentum or Devil May Cry's combo styling than a cover shooter

Hands-on previews describe stringing together light attacks, heavy finishers, and supernatural abilities while constantly moving through wide arenas. Enemies traverse the environment as fast as you do, so standing still is not an option.

We break down all five confirmed Aberrant forms — Dual Blades, Axe, Scythe, Whip, and Heavy Hammer — in our [Aberrant weapon guide](/games/control-resonant/aberrant-weapon-forms).

## 3. From the Oldest House to Warped Manhattan

The first *Control* was claustrophobic by design: a brutalist concrete labyrinth where the architecture itself shifted around you. Resonant opens the doors and takes the paranatural chaos outside.

Manhattan has been twisted and folded by supernatural forces — gravity distorts, skyscrapers bend, and the streets are overrun with anomalies. Instead of one interconnected HQ, the game is structured around large **Extraction Zones** across New York City, packed with side activities, hidden lore, and optional red-circle encounters, with more linear missions threaded through for the main story.

The contrast is intentional: Jesse's game was about containment inside a bunker; Dylan's is about a city that can no longer be contained.

## 4. It's an Action-RPG Now

*Control* had light RPG elements — weapon mods, ability upgrades, personal mods. Resonant commits fully to the genre label:

- **Skill trees** for each Aberrant form and each supernatural ability
- **Build-crafting** with artifacts that alter how your kit plays
- **Free respec**, so you can experiment without penalty
- A **New Game+** mode that carries progression between cycles

The combat loop is built around the **Gap system** — a stagger mechanic that structures every fight from opening moves to finish. Preparation matters: builds that click with your playstyle are described as a "huge part of the experience." Our [combat systems preview](/games/control-resonant/combat-systems-preview) covers the Gap system, respec, and NG+ in detail.

## 5. The Hiss Are Out of Containment

In *Control*, the Hiss invaded the Oldest House and mostly stayed there — the Ordinary AWE aside, the world outside was normal. Resonant removes that safety net: the breach has spread to the city itself.

Dylan also isn't fighting alone in narrative terms. New factions appear — some hostile, some not — and enemies bring paranatural attacks and environmental traversal that the possessed FBC agents of the first game never had.

## 6. Difficulty and Accessibility: A Different Philosophy

*Control* shipped with a single difficulty curve that was famously punishing in its early hours, later softened by an Assist Mode patch that let players tweak aim assist, enemy damage, and one-hit kills.

Resonant builds that philosophy in from day one. The PlayStation Store listing confirms **Adjustable Difficulty (Advanced)** and **Game Pausing**, and Remedy has confirmed New Game+ includes higher-difficulty options. Our [difficulty settings guide](/games/control-resonant/difficulty-settings) has the full breakdown.

## 7. Bigger Scope, Longer Game

*Control* ran roughly 11–13 hours for the main story and around 25–30 hours for a completionist run including *Foundation* and *AWE*. Resonant is a bigger production: current estimates land at **~30 hours for the main story and ~50 hours for full completion** — roughly double the original. The [full playtime breakdown is here](/games/control-resonant/how-long-to-beat).

## 8. A New Engine Generation

Both games run on Remedy's Northlight engine, but Resonant's version has been overhauled for vast outdoor spaces: path tracing support, more complex material rendering, and the tech needed to make a ruined Manhattan bend and fold in real time. If your PC handled *Control* at max settings, don't assume the same for Resonant — check the [expected system requirements](/games/control-resonant/system-requirements).

## What Stays the Same

For all the changes, this is unmistakably Control's universe:

- **The Federal Bureau of Control** — same bureau, new crisis
- **The Board** — still speaking in inverted platitudes, still manifesting the weapon
- **Objects of Power and altered items** — the paranatural collectibles are back
- **Remedy's connected universe** — expect the usual winks toward Alan Wake
- **The dense, breadcrumb lore delivery** — documents, recordings, and corrupted text everywhere

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is Control Resonant a sequel or a reboot?

It's a continuation — set roughly seven years after *Control* — but Remedy calls it a "sibling" to the first game rather than a direct sequel. You play a different Faden sibling, in a different borough of reality, with a different weapon. It's not a reboot; the events of *Control* absolutely happened and matter.

### Do I need to play Control (2019) first?

No. Remedy designed Resonant so newcomers can start here — the character Zoe exists specifically to have the lore explained to her. But playing *Control* first (or reading our [story recap](/games/control-resonant/story-recap)) makes the character arcs far more meaningful.

### Is Jesse Faden in Control Resonant?

Jesse is missing ("gone fishing") at the start of the game, and finding her is part of Dylan's motivation. Whether she appears in person — Remedy hasn't spoiled that.

### Is Control Resonant an open-world game?

Not a seamless open world. It's structured as large, interconnected Extraction Zones across Manhattan, with linear missions for the main story. It's far more open than the Oldest House ever was.

### Will Control Resonant feel like Control?

Tonally, yes — the same bureaucratic-creepy Remedy DNA. Mechanically, no — melee-focused Action-RPG combat replaces ranged gunplay. If you loved *Control* for its atmosphere and lore, you'll feel at home. If you loved it for the shooting, prepare for a very different game.

## The Bottom Line

*Control* was a contained, atmospheric shooter about one building. *Control Resonant* is an aggressive, build-driven Action-RPG about a broken city. Same universe, same developer weirdness, almost entirely new rules.

Whether that's an upgrade depends on what you loved about the original — but there's no question Remedy is taking the kind of swing sequels rarely take. September 24, 2026 will tell us if it lands.
