Pre-ordering Control Resonant is a simple yes/no question wrapped in a $10 decision. The base game costs **$59.99**; the Deluxe edition costs **$69.99**. Both come with the **48-hour PS5 early access** perk. The real question is whether the Deluxe bonuses justify the extra ten dollars — and this guide breaks down exactly what you get so you can decide before September 22, when the early access window opens.

<ProTip title="The early access is on both editions">

## Deluxe Edition Bonuses, Explained

Here's what the extra $10 buys, and whether each item actually matters:

**🎨 "Resonant Surge" Aberrant skin pack** — Five weapon-form skins for the Aberrant (one per [form](/games/control-resonant/aberrant-weapon-forms)), each with a glowing alternate palette. Cosmetic only, but if you plan to main the Aberrant for 30+ hours, variety is nice.

**👕 Dylan Faden alternate outfit** — A second outfit for the protagonist. Purely cosmetic. This is the "Moses in the desert" drip option.

**📖 Digital artbook & soundtrack** — The artbook covers the game's "warped Manhattan" concept work, and the soundtrack runs ~40 tracks. For Remedy fans who already own the *Control* artbook, this slots right next to it.

**⚡ Launch weapon-mastery booster** — A modest early-game progression boost for the Aberrant. Based on how these bonuses usually work, it accelerates your first few hours of [weapon mastery](/games/control-resonant/combat-systems-preview) — a small head start, not a pay-to-win lever.

## The 48-Hour PS5 Early Access, in Practical Terms

This is the headline pre-order perk, so let's be precise about what it means:

- **Any pre-order** (Standard or Deluxe) on PS5 gets early access starting **September 22, 2026**.
- The **worldwide launch** is September 24 on PS5, Xbox, and PC.
- Xbox and PC players do **not** get early access — it's a PS5-exclusive perk.

Why does it matter? Three reasons: you get two extra days before the internet floods with guides, you avoid day-one queue issues, and if the game has a strong first-weekend meta, you're two days ahead of it. If you're on PS5 and you know you're buying the game anyway, there's no reason *not* to pre-order.

<InfoBox title="Refund note">
PlayStation refunds for pre-ordered digital titles are generally available up to the release date. If you're on the fence, you can pre-order for the early access slot and still have a window to change your mind before September 24 — check the PlayStation Store refund policy for the exact terms in your region.
</InfoBox>

## Should You Pre-Order Control Resonant?

Let's be honest, the way a guide should be:

**Pre-order if...**
- You're on PS5 and want the 48-hour early access. This is the single strongest reason.

## FAQ

### Does the Standard edition include early access?
Yes. Both Standard ($59.99) and Deluxe ($69.99) pre-orders on PS5 include the 48-hour early access window.

### What's the difference between Standard and Deluxe?
The Deluxe edition adds the "Resonant Surge" Aberrant skin pack, an alternate Dylan outfit, the digital artbook + soundtrack, and a launch weapon-mastery booster. The game itself is identical.

### When does early access start?
September 22, 2026 on PS5. The worldwide launch follows on September 24, 2026.

### Can I get early access on Xbox or PC?
No. The 48-hour early access is a PlayStation 5 exclusive pre-order perk.

### Will Deluxe cosmetics be sold separately later?
Almost certainly — cosmetic packs and artbooks from Remedy's previous titles have been made available separately after launch. If you're patient, the Deluxe's extras will likely be purchasable on their own.

### Is Control Resonant pre-order available on Mac?
The Mac version of the game arrives later in 2026, and pre-orders for it are not live yet.

## The Verdict

Pre-ordering on **PS5 is a no-brainer if you're buying anyway** — the free 48-hour early access is a real benefit. On Xbox and PC, pre-ordering is a neutral act; you're not losing anything by waiting for day-one impressions, and the Deluxe's $10 of cosmetics is the only thing tempting you early. If you do go Deluxe, know exactly what you're paying for: skins, a soundtrack, an artbook, and a tiny head start — nothing that changes whether the game is worth it.
