Every piece of Control Resonant marketing has one visual at its center: **the Aberrant**, the shapeshifting weapon that Dylan Faden wields across a warped Manhattan. It's the sequel's answer to Jesse Faden's Service Weapon — but where the Service Weapon was a gun that changed form, the Aberrant is a melee weapon that changes its entire combat identity on command.

This guide breaks down all five confirmed forms, what each one is good at, and how to think about them before you even touch the game.

## What Is the Aberrant?

The Aberrant is Dylan Faden's signature weapon — a living, morphing instrument of his resonance abilities. Lore-wise, it's a direct evolution of the powers Dylan displayed at the end of the first game, weaponized into something he can bring to a street fight in Manhattan.

Mechanically, the Aberrant is a **single weapon with five forms**, and you swap between them mid-combat. Each form changes your move set, attack speed, range, and crowd-control profile. Think of it as five weapons in one slot — your build and your form choices are two halves of the same decision.

## 1. Dual Blades — The Versatile Crowd-Clearer

**Playstyle:** Fast, wide-sweeping attacks with rapid follow-ups. Dual Blades are the form you default to when you're surrounded by the Hiss-adjacent thralls that swarm the streets of Manhattan. The moveset emphasizes area coverage over single-target damage — you're a blender, not a scalpel.

**Strengths:**
- Fastest attack speed of all five forms — great for applying status effects and building the Gap meter
- Wide arcs hit multiple enemies at once
- Low commitment: you can cancel into dodges easily

**Weaknesses:**
- Lowest per-hit damage
- Short reach — you have to be in the middle of the fight

**When to use it:** General traversal combat, trash mobs, and any situation where you're outnumbered. If you're unsure which form to start a fight with, Dual Blades is the safe opener.

## 2. Axe — The Stagger Machine

**Playstyle:** Slow, heavy, and absolutely devastating when it lands. The Axe is the Aberrant's power form. Charged attacks are the headline — a fully charged Axe swing is the most reliable way in the game to stagger or flat-out knock over mid-tier enemies, which opens them up for the combat system's [Gap finishers](/games/control-resonant/combat-systems-preview).

**Strengths:**
- Highest per-hit damage in the weapon's kit
- Massive stagger damage — the Gap system's best friend
- Armor-breaking potential against elites

**Weaknesses:**
- Slow windup leaves you exposed — you must read enemy attacks before committing
- Terrible in crowds; the narrow, heavy swings don't clear groups

**When to use it:** Bosses, elites, and armored enemies. The Axe is a *punish* weapon — wait for an enemy's attack to whiff, then answer with a charged swing. It's the form that rewards game knowledge over reflexes.

## 3. Scythe — The Combo Driver

**Playstyle:** The Scythe sits in the middle of the Aberrant's stat profile — medium speed, medium range, high combo potential. It's the form for players who like flow. Scythe attacks chain into each other smoothly, and its wide horizontal sweeps let you control space while still dealing real damage. It also has the best mobility tools in the kit, with dashes woven into the attack strings.

**Strengths:**
- Best combo potential — longest attack chains in the weapon
- Balanced profile that works in nearly any fight
- Excellent repositioning mid-combo

**Weaknesses:**
- Jack of all trades, master of none — no standout specialty
- Less stagger than the Axe, less coverage than Dual Blades

**When to use it:** Any fight where you want consistent pressure without committing to a specialist form. If you're building around [form-swap combos](/games/control-resonant/combat-systems-preview), the Scythe is usually the form you flow *through* — swap in, extend the combo, swap out.

## 4. Whip — The Ranged Control Tool

**Playstyle:** The Whip turns the Aberrant into a ranged weapon. It's the form with real reach — lashing attacks that hit from a safe distance, a charged attack that pulls enemies toward you, and sweeping arcs that keep crowds at bay. It's the only form that lets you engage without committing to melee range.

**Strengths:**
- Longest reach in the kit
- Pull mechanics — yank enemies out of cover or out of packs
- Great at interrupting enemy attack animations from range

**Weaknesses:**
- Lowest sustained DPS of the five
- Precision-dependent: missed attacks leave you exposed

**When to use it:** Enemies with dangerous close-range attacks, flying enemies, and pack management. The Whip is also the form you want when you're learning a new enemy's moveset — you can study from range without eating the lesson.

## 5. Heavy Hammer — The Devastator

**Playstyle:** The Heavy Hammer is the Aberrant's slowest, heaviest, most destructive form. Where the Axe staggers, the Hammer *shatters* — its attacks are built around breaking enemy armor and staggering windows outright. It's the form for the "if it's not dead in one hit, it's about to be" approach.

**Strengths:**
- Highest single-hit damage in the entire weapon
- Best armor-break potential — the dedicated elite-killer form
- Devastating against staggered enemies (massive damage bonus)

**Weaknesses:**
- Slowest attacks in the kit — the most punishable form if you whiff
- Minimal crowd control; you're committed to a single target

**When to use it:** Elites, mini-bosses, and any enemy with an armor bar. The Hammer is the finisher form — soften enemies up with another form, then swap to Hammer to execute.

<ProTip title="Form-swap timing">
Swapping forms mid-combo costs no extra time in Control Resonant — the morph is instant. The most effective play pattern we've seen described is: open with Dual Blades or Whip to build the Gap meter safely, then swap to Axe or Hammer the moment the enemy is staggered for a massive punish. Master the swap, and the Aberrant becomes five weapons, not one.
</ProTip>

## FAQ

### How do I switch Aberrant forms in Control Resonant?
Form swapping is instant and can be done mid-combo — Remedy has emphasized that the morph itself costs no time, so swapping to the right form for the moment is the core combat skill.

### Which Aberrant form is the best?
For launch, the Heavy Hammer and Axe rank highest because both feed directly into the Gap finisher system with stagger and armor-break damage. The Scythe is the best generalist for players who don't want to swap constantly.

### Is the Abernant a gun like the Service Weapon?
No. The Service Weapon from Control was a shapeshifting *gun*. The Aberrant is a shapeshifting *melee* weapon with five forms: Dual Blades, Axe, Scythe, Whip, and Heavy Hammer.

### Do the forms level up separately?
Weapon mastery in Control Resonant is form-aware — progress on each form contributes to your overall Aberrant mastery, and the [combat systems preview](/games/control-resonant/combat-systems-preview) covers how the skill tree interacts with forms.

### Can I change forms mid-fight?
Yes — instant swapping mid-combat is the core of the weapon's design.

## The Bottom Line

The Aberrant is Control Resonant's most important system — it's your weapon, your build, and your playstyle in one morphing package. Dual Blades clear, Axe staggers, Scythe flows, Whip controls, and Hammer destroys. No single form is "the best" for every fight; the skill ceiling lives in knowing which form the *moment* demands.
